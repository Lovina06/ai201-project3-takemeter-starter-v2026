# Label Design - Milestone 2

## The Three Labels

### 1. ANALYSIS
**Definition:** Makes an argument backed by at least one specific, checkable fact. Facts include: box office numbers, release dates, production details, named crew/cast, critical scores, specific plot points, technical details (runtime, budget, etc.).

**Example 1:**
"Dune Part Two made $82M in its opening weekend, which is impressive considering the first film only made $41M. The studio clearly built better word-of-mouth this time, and the expanded IMAX release helped - IMAX alone accounted for $28M of that total."

**Example 2:**
"The Substance uses body horror in the Cronenberg tradition - both films feature slow physical transformation as metaphor for social pressure. The makeup effects here rival Videodrome's practical work, which is saying something given today's CGI shortcuts."

### 2. HOT_TAKE
**Definition:** A confident claim or judgment with NO specific supporting evidence. May be true, may be insightful, but isn't argued - just stated. Typically uses strong language ("best," "worst," "masterpiece," "garbage") without backing it up.

**Example 1:**
"Wicked is genuinely one of the best musicals ever put to film. The performances, the visuals, everything works. If you disagree you probably just don't like musicals."

**Example 2:**
"Anyone saying The Wild Robot is mid clearly has no taste. That movie hits different and if you know you know."

### 3. DISCUSSION
**Definition:** Explicitly invites others to weigh in. May ask a question, request recommendations, seek interpretation help, or present multiple viewpoints and ask which is right. The PURPOSE is conversation, not declaration.

**Example 1:**
"I just finished Anora and I'm genuinely unsure if the ending is hopeful or devastating. What did you all take from it? Was she better off or worse?"

**Example 2:**
"Looking for movies with unreliable narrators like Shutter Island or Fight Club. Preferably where the twist actually changes how you see the whole story, not just tacked on. What hits that mark for you?"

## Borderline Case and Decision Rule

**The Hard Boundary: ANALYSIS vs HOT_TAKE when tone is strong**

**Borderline Post:**
"Oppenheimer is Nolan's best work. The sound design alone puts it above Dunkirk - every explosion feels visceral in a way Dunkirk's beach scenes didn't. The 70mm IMAX presentation is essential."

**Why it's borderline:**
- Strong declarative claim ("best work") sounds like HOT_TAKE
- But mentions specific comparisons (Dunkirk), technical details (sound design, 70mm IMAX, explosions vs beach scenes)

**Decision Rule:**
**If the post names at least one specific, checkable fact (a comparison to another named work, a technical spec, a named person/number/date), it's ANALYSIS even if the tone is confident.** 

Hot takes are ONLY for posts with zero specific grounding - pure opinion statements.

**Therefore this example → ANALYSIS** (compares to Dunkirk specifically, mentions sound design and 70mm IMAX format)
