# Hard Cases - Labeling Challenges

## Case 1: Strong Opinion + Specific Fact
**Post:** "Oppenheimer is Nolan's best work. The sound design alone puts it above Dunkirk - every explosion feels visceral in a way Dunkirk's beach scenes didn't. The 70mm IMAX presentation is essential."

**Candidates:** ANALYSIS vs HOT_TAKE

**Why hard:** Opens with declarative "best work" claim (HOT_TAKE signal) but immediately supports it with specific comparisons (sound design vs Dunkirk, 70mm IMAX format).

**Decision:** ANALYSIS
**Rule applied:** If post names at least one specific, checkable fact (comparison to named work, technical spec, named person/date/number), it's ANALYSIS even if tone is confident. The mentions of Dunkirk specifically, sound design comparison, and 70mm IMAX format made it analysis.

---

## Case 2: Question + Embedded Opinion
**Post:** "Does anyone else think A24 horror is getting too pretentious? Like I appreciate slow burns but at what point does artsy become boring? Where's the line for you?"

**Candidates:** DISCUSSION vs HOT_TAKE

**Why hard:** Contains strong opinion about A24 horror being "pretentious" and "boring" (HOT_TAKE language) but framed as questions seeking others' input.

**Decision:** DISCUSSION
**Rule applied:** If the PURPOSE is conversation (ends with questions, explicitly seeks input), it's DISCUSSION even if opinions are expressed. The "Does anyone else think..." framing and "Where's the line for you?" explicitly invite response.

## Case 3: Facts Presented Neutrally vs Argumentatively
**Post:** "Dune Part Two made $82M in its opening weekend, which is impressive considering the first film only made $41M. The studio clearly built better word-of-mouth this time, and the expanded IMAX release helped - IMAX alone accounted for $28M of that total."

**Candidates:** ANALYSIS (certain)

**Why it seemed easy but wasn't:** All candidates correctly labeled this ANALYSIS, but the interesting question was whether "clearly built better word-of-mouth" makes it opinion-based. 

**Why still ANALYSIS:** The claim is supported by the doubling of box office ($41M→$82M) and specific IMAX numbers ($28M). The word "clearly" points to evidence, not just feeling.

---

## Case 4: Vague Comparisons
**Post:** "The Wild Robot is genuinely one of the best animated films I've seen in years. Every frame is gorgeous and the emotional beats land perfectly."

**Candidates:** HOT_TAKE (certain)

**Why considered:** Says "best...in years" which could imply comparison, but doesn't name what films it's beating. "Every frame" and "emotional beats" are evaluative claims with no specific evidence.

**Decision:** HOT_TAKE
**Rule:** Comparisons must be SPECIFIC (named works, actual numbers) to count as evidence. "In years" is too vague.

---

## Case 5: Seeking Recommendations vs Seeking Discussion
**Post:** "Looking for movies with unreliable narrators like Shutter Island or Fight Club. Preferably where the twist actually changes how you see the whole story, not just tacked on. What hits that mark for you?"

**Candidates:** DISCUSSION (certain)

**Why straightforward:** Explicitly asks for recommendations ("Looking for...") and ends with question. Clear DISCUSSION.

**Learning:** Not all DISCUSSION posts are philosophical debates. Recommendation requests that invite sharing ("What hits that mark for you?") are also DISCUSSION because the purpose is conversation/gathering input.

---

## Summary of Labeling Challenges

The hardest boundary was **ANALYSIS vs HOT_TAKE when strong opinions meet evidence**. The key distinction became: does the post name at least one checkable fact (specific film title, number, date, person, technical detail)? If yes → ANALYSIS. If no → HOT_TAKE.

The **DISCUSSION category was most clear** because it hinges on PURPOSE (seeking input) rather than content. Even posts with strong opinions became DISCUSSION if they explicitly invited response.

**Consistency check:** On the 20 cold-labeled posts, I applied these rules without AI assistance and felt confident in 18/20. The 2 uncertain ones both sat on the ANALYSIS/HOT_TAKE line and I chose based on whether specific facts were named.
