# Milestone 1: Community Selection and Initial Observations

## Community: r/movies

**Why this community:**
- Public subreddit with high volume of text posts
- Real variation in post quality and depth
- Discusses movies generally (not one specific film, avoiding spoilers)
- Mix of casual fans and serious film enthusiasts

## Candidate Distinctions (from reading)

### 1. Evidence-backed vs Unsupported Claims
- **What I observed**: Some posts cite specific facts (box office numbers, production details, director quotes, critical consensus scores). Others make strong claims with no supporting evidence.
- **Example**: "The movie flopped because it only made $12M opening weekend on a $80M budget" vs "This movie is a masterpiece"

### 2. Analytical vs Reactive
- **What I observed**: Some posts develop structured arguments about themes, cinematography, narrative choices. Others are immediate emotional responses.
- **Example**: "The use of red lighting in act two foreshadows the protagonist's moral descent" vs "Just finished watching and WOW that ending!"

### 3. Question-Driven vs Declarative
- **What I observed**: Some posts invite discussion with open questions. Others make confident statements.
- **Example**: "What do you think the director intended with that ambiguous ending?" vs "The ending makes no sense and ruins the whole film"

### 4. Depth and Development
- **What I observed**: Length correlates with depth - multi-paragraph posts tend to develop ideas, while one-liners tend to be hot takes.
- **Example**: 300-word analysis of pacing vs "Best movie of the year, fight me"

## Moving forward to labels

Based on these observations, I'll design 3 labels that capture the most meaningful distinctions:
1. **Analysis** - Evidence-backed, developed argument
2. **Hot Take** - Strong opinion, no evidence, often declarative
3. **Discussion** - Question-driven, seeking input, may or may not have depth
